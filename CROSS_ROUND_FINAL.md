# CROSS-ROUND FINAL TOURNAMENT

Last updated: 2026-08-15
Status: **COMPLETE — C13 Organism Cargo selected**

This document is the final destructive comparison across the six surviving concepts. It does not declare the overall project complete. It closes Phase 2 and provides the evidence for selecting exactly one product concept for Phase 3.

---

# 1. Final decision standard

A winner must satisfy all of the following, not merely have the highest average score:

1. the mechanic remains interesting when theme/art/lore are stripped away;
2. the theme materially increases desire rather than hiding a weak mechanic;
3. the first minute contains a pleasurable legible action;
4. the game can teach its rules incrementally without requiring a manual;
5. a primitive deterministic graybox can validate the core cheaply;
6. a Steam demo can expose the hook without exhausting the whole game;
7. the content model scales through combinations rather than hundreds of bespoke assets;
8. current market comparisons do not collapse the pitch into a known product;
9. implementation can be specified deterministically enough for another session to build;
10. the production ceiling remains compatible with a compact AI-assisted indie project;
11. there is a credible source of fresh decisions after 10+ hours;
12. there is no fatal kill-threshold already triggered by paper analysis.

A fatal failure cannot be averaged away by strengths elsewhere.

# 4. Theme-removal tests

## C13 — Organism Cargo
Replace creatures with anonymous tokens carrying traits such as `produces heat`, `grows on tick 4`, `calms adjacent`, `consumes gas`, `infects neighbor`, `needs light`.

The decision remains intact:
- choose an initial spatial state;
- predict time-dependent interactions;
- commit;
- observe deterministic ticks;
- explain a cascade;
- revise.

**Result: PASS — strong.** The game still has a real systems puzzle without creature charm.

## C21 — Machine Exorcist
Replace haunted parts with normal graph nodes plus a second family of hidden rule modifiers that can alter measurement truth, adjacency, memory, or state propagation.

The diagnostic game remains interesting, but the pitch loses a large amount of emotional identity and spectacle.

**Result: PASS — strong mechanic, theme carries unusually high desire value.**

## C24 — Inventory Reactor
Replace reactor modules with abstract colored nodes and ports.

Spatial prediction and deterministic cascading remain highly legible. The concept passes mechanically but starts looking like an abstract optimization puzzle.

**Result: PASS — very strong mechanic, weak noun independence at market layer.**

## C12 — Micro-Factory Troubleshooter
Replace factory components with generic graph nodes.

Diagnosis/minimum intervention remains logically valid, but the activity approaches software/debugging work very quickly.

**Result: PASS mechanically / WARNING emotionally.**

## C27 — Creature Lab Triage
Replace creatures with hidden-state cards, tests, and interventions.

The information-acquisition problem survives, but emotional investment drops more sharply than C13 and optimal-play risk (“buy enough information”) becomes more visible.

**Result: CONDITIONAL PASS.**

## C23 — Dream Cartographer
Replace impossible rooms with abstract nodes and hidden transition rules.

The mechanic becomes manual model-building and falsification. Some players may love it, but the clerical-risk kill condition becomes central.

**Result: WEAK PASS / HIGH RISK.**

# 5. Theme-replacement tests

This asks whether the core is robust enough to survive different nouns, while identifying which theme best amplifies desire.

## C13 underlying mechanic
Possible replacements:
- unstable chemicals in a courier crate;
- alien plants in a greenhouse transport pod;
- small robots whose behaviors trigger one another;
- magical artifacts changing states during shipment;
- living organisms.

All preserve the prepare → simulate → diagnose loop. **Living organisms are best** because they make growth, feeding, panic, infection, sleep, symbiosis, and welfare intuitive rather than arbitrary. The theme teaches the system and generates stories.

**Result: EXCELLENT theme/mechanic fit.**

## C21 underlying mechanic
Could become cursed artifacts, impossible appliances, alien machinery, or magical instruments. The diagnostic core survives. Haunted machines remain strong because “normal mechanical logic vs supernatural law” is the whole contrast.

**Result: EXCELLENT, but requires more authored presentation than C13.**

## C24 underlying mechanic
Could be a power core, ship subsystem, alchemical machine, spell engine, or industrial controller. Mechanic survives easily, which proves robustness but also exposes that the current reactor fantasy is replaceable and therefore not itself a product moat.

**Result: STRONG mechanic / theme still unresolved.**

## C12 underlying mechanic
Could be logistics, data flow, plumbing, circuits, or miniature production. Minimum-intervention diagnosis survives. Factory is readable but not especially desire-generating.

**Result: STRONG mechanic / weak emotional multiplier.**

## C27 underlying mechanic
Could be robot repair, magical diagnosis, artifact testing, or alien biology. Creature biology remains best because symptoms and care create attachment, but this also raises art/animation expectations.

**Result: GOOD theme fit, higher production dependence.**

## C23 underlying mechanic
Could be a dream, impossible archive, portal network, or time maze. The mechanic remains but the theme does substantial work to make note-taking feel like exploration.

**Result: CONDITIONAL.**

# 6. Primitive graybox validation contracts

These are validation contracts, not production implementation instructions.

## C13
- 5x5 grid;
- 8–10 token organisms;
- 10 modular traits;
- 3 environmental channels: heat, stress, contamination;
- growth and feeding state changes;
- 12 deterministic ticks;
- event/causal log;
- 12 handcrafted solvable manifests;
- no art beyond icons/colors.

**Pass threshold:** after the player understands all current rules, at least 70% of failed shipments in a small playtest should produce an articulated causal explanation and a specific planned revision rather than random rearrangement. At least half of interesting failures must depend on a state change after launch, not static adjacency.

**Kill threshold:** static pre-launch inspection solves most cases, or transit is watched as spectacle rather than used as evidence.

## C21
- 8-slot machine graph;
- 10 parts;
- 4 ordinary fault rules;
- 4 abnormal law modifiers;
- 5 tests;
- 3 interventions;
- deterministic power test;
- 12 cases.

**Pass threshold:** players distinguish at least two mechanically identical-looking symptoms using targeted tests and can explain why the selected intervention follows.

**Kill threshold:** broad testing/replacement beats hypothesis-driven play, or occult laws feel arbitrary after being learned.

## C24
- 6x6 grid;
- 12 modules;
- power, heat, interference;
- rotations/directional ports;
- deterministic pulse simulation;
- causal trace;
- 10 contracts.

**Pass threshold:** players form predictions about dynamic consequences and revise based on causal trace.

**Kill threshold:** reusable layout templates dominate or brute-force placement is as effective as understanding.

## C12
- node/link graph;
- 8 components;
- 8 injected faults;
- deterministic ticks;
- intervention counter;
- 15 known-answer cases.

**Pass threshold:** observation/diagnosis is consistently reported as satisfying rather than work, and minimum-intervention solutions create “aha” moments.

**Kill threshold:** players would rather rebuild freely, or fault tracing feels like unpaid debugging.

## C27
- specimen panel;
- 8 traits;
- 5 conditions;
- 5 tests;
- 6 treatments;
- deterministic reaction matrix;
- 20 known-answer cases.

**Pass threshold:** optimal play chooses tests by expected information and sometimes acts under bounded uncertainty.

**Kill threshold:** run-all-tests dominates or creature art is required before the decision loop becomes enjoyable.

## C23
- 12 abstract rooms;
- 4 exit symbols;
- 4 topology laws;
- constrained node-map annotations;
- 8 scenarios.

**Pass threshold:** players voluntarily predict untraversed routes using the model.

**Kill threshold:** brute-force traversal is preferred or map work feels clerical.

# 7. Fatal-threshold screen

## C23 — eliminated
Paper evidence does not prove the literal graybox is bad, but it fails the production-confidence threshold compared with the leaders: fair inferability, generator validation, and mapping UX are all difficult simultaneously, while first-minute pleasure and clip legibility are weaker.

**Decision: ELIMINATE.** Preserve predictive-model and falsification ideas as possible challenge-design inspiration.

## C27 — eliminated from product final
No fatal flaw, but it is dominated by C13/C21 on the combination of emotional identity + systemic consequence + visual story generation. It also has a stronger risk that expressive creature art is necessary before the graybox feels desirable.

**Decision: ELIMINATE FROM FINAL PRODUCT BRACKET.** Preserve limited-information testing and “do not buy all information” principles.

## C12 — eliminated from product final
This remains the safest engineering prototype and the best procedural solvability template. However, its emotional-desire and first-minute scores remain meaningfully below the top three. The project should borrow its known-good-state → inject-fault → validate-solution methodology instead of shipping the factory/debugging fantasy.

**Decision: ELIMINATE FROM FINAL PRODUCT BRACKET.**

# 9. Pairwise final

## C13 vs C24
C24 is easier to implement and validate, but C13's theme naturally teaches why state changes happen: living things grow, eat, sleep, infect, panic, soothe, and react to environment. Those verbs provide dynamic depth without feeling like arbitrary engineering exceptions. C13 also creates stronger stories and more visually readable failures/recoveries.

C24's advantage is cleaner abstraction and lower art cost. Its disadvantage is that adjacency/board strategy and reactor terminology already have nearby market gravity. It would need unusually strong presentation to avoid reading as “another compact systems puzzle.”

**Winner: C13.**

## C13 vs C21
C21 has the strongest single fantasy sentence and perhaps the strongest authored milestone moments. It also requires players to learn two models at once: normal machinery and supernatural laws. Good cases need diagnostic distinguishability, modular machines need readable personality, and the supernatural layer must never feel arbitrary. This is achievable but creates a larger tutorial/design/presentation obligation.

C13 can express equivalent second-order complexity through intuitive biology and transit: initial layout is visibly transformed over time, creatures can compose from reusable traits, and the same deterministic simulation drives both puzzle depth and spectacle. It therefore offers a better ratio of emotional identity to implementation/specification clarity.

**Winner: C13, narrowly.**

## C21 vs C24
C21 wins desire/identity; C24 wins implementation simplicity. Since C13 already beats both on balance, no tie-break is needed.

# 10. Final concept decision

## SELECTED: C13 — ORGANISM CARGO

Working product thesis:

> **A compact deterministic strategy/puzzle game where the player packs living cargo into a constrained transport hold, commits to launch, then watches organisms change state and interact through a short transit simulation; success comes from predicting the future ecology, explaining cascades, and improving the initial arrangement — not from satisfying static packing rules.**

The name `Organism Cargo` remains a working codename, not a locked commercial title.

## Why this concept wins

- mechanic survives without theme;
- creature theme makes the mechanics more intuitive and desirable rather than merely decorating them;
- dynamic transit cleanly differentiates it from static packing comparisons;
- one compact board/hold keeps environmental scope controlled;
- modular traits can generate many organisms and situations from a limited asset library;
- deterministic simulation supports learning, causal explanation, challenge validation, replays, seeded challenges, and implementation clarity;
- visible state changes create immediate trailer/stream value;
- failures naturally generate short stories;
- demo boundary is clean;
- progression can expand knowledge and interaction vocabulary rather than rely on grind;
- it supports the project's preferred rhythm: inspect → hypothesize → prepare → commit → observe → explain → improve.

## Non-negotiable differentiation rule

**The hold is not solved when the doors close.**

If a challenge can usually be solved as a static adjacency/shape puzzle before launch, it violates the selected thesis. Transit must cause meaningful, deterministic, learnable changes that alter relationships after commitment.

## Borrowed strengths from eliminated finalists

These are design ingredients, not extra feature sets:
- from C12: generate from a known-valid state and validate solvability;
- from C27: limited information can create optional advanced contracts, but exhaustive testing must never dominate;
- from C24: causal overlays and deterministic post-run trace;
- from C21: expressive, memorable failure/recovery behavior;
- from C25: commitment under incomplete information and cascade graphs, used sparingly;
- from C23: predictions should sometimes be provable before the player has witnessed the exact event.
